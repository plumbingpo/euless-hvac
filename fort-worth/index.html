The emergency calls we answer tend to follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's an AC blowing warm air, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil iced over. Year-round, it's breakers that trip every cycle, plugged condensate drains backing water into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that stop responding. Because our trucks carry capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, the majority of these calls are wrapped up in one visit, at a price you approve before we start.

A little caution before we arrive can prevent a much bigger repair. An iced-over AC should be shut off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw faster. Running it frozen risks an overflowing drain pan and a damaged compressor. A furnace that keeps short-cycling, or any burning smell, means it's time to kill power at the furnace switch and call us right away.

Keeping Your Fort Worth HVAC System Running Longer

Routine maintenance is the difference between a system that lasts and one that breaks down in the middle of a heat wave. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a new filter every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and a professional tune-up every spring and fall, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners run 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and showing symptoms, such as rising energy bills, rooms that never feel comfortable, or a service call every season, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one in the middle of a cold snap.

Small problems don't stay minor for long. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but left alone for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a miserable night.
